# Validation record

Executed locally on 22 September 2026.

## Automated game rules — passed

`npm test` passes five tests:

1. A fresh chapter can be completed, fully decorated, and round-tripped through the save loader with the expected balances.
2. Repeat discoveries, restorations, gift claims, and purchases cannot duplicate rewards or spend twice.
3. Repair requirements, story order, unavailable purchases, and the completion-gift condition are enforced without losing resources.
4. Malformed, unknown-version, and inconsistent saves recover safely; known identifiers and finite coordinate bounds are respected.
5. Buying a cosmetic early cannot consume the resources needed to complete the story.

The final tested economy is 132 wonder, 145 earned buttons including the gift, 75 spent on all decorations, and 70 buttons remaining. All nine repair threads are consumed by the three keepsakes.

## Browser playthrough — passed

`npm run test:browser` uses Playwright with installed Google Chrome. The suite exercises actual clicks, keyboard presses, and emulated touch, not direct mutation of the game state.

- Pointer navigation to a world item and automatic proximity collection.
- Visiting the bear before its story prerequisite: visible explanation and a disabled repair button.
- Guided paths around furniture, resource collection, all three restorations, and their memory scenes.
- Chapter ending, one completion gift, and all three visible decoration purchases.
- Persistent discoveries, restores, currency, and decorations after reloading.
- Replaying a memory without additional rewards; opening the discovery collection.
- Reduced decorative motion and sound toggle persistence.
- Keyboard movement and PNG photo download.
- Fresh mobile session with touch collection and decoration menus.
- Viewports of 1440×1000, 390×844, and 360×640, with no horizontal overflow at the mobile sizes.
- No uncaught JavaScript errors or failed HTTP responses during the suite.

The first browser run caught a pointer-hit issue with the floating keepsake buttons. Their stacking order and active transform were fixed; the complete suite passed afterward.

## Performance observations

Static geometry batching reduced Three.js's reported main-render draw calls from 476 to 223 in the initial desktop room. The scene contains about 141,000 rendered triangles in that view. These are local diagnostic observations, not a frame-rate guarantee. Pixel ratio is capped at 1.7. Real-device GPU time, battery impact, memory, thermal behavior, and low-end hardware have not been profiled.

## Practical limits

- One completed room and one story chapter. The proposed four later chapters and ability-based growth are not implemented.
- Browser-local saving only; no cloud sync or account system.
- Original procedural assets and synthesized audio, rather than final authored animation and studio audio.
- Phone checks use browser emulation, not physical iOS/Android devices. Safari and Firefox were not tested in this delivery.
- Keyboard alternatives, native HTML dialogs, focus indicators, announcements, and reduced motion are provided. A comprehensive accessibility audit and screen-reader playthrough remain outstanding.
- The local Node server is for development and review. There is no public deployment or native package.
- Proposed playtime, audience appeal, retention, and willingness to pay have not been validated with users.

Next milestone: observe target-audience players, prioritize their actual points of confusion, then decide whether to invest in production character art and a second room.
